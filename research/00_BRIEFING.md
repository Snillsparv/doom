# PROJECT BRIEFING (read fully before starting)

## Context
A Swedish YouTube creator is making a short, pedagogical video series answering the question
ordinary people ask after recent AI news (Jacob Coxon resigned from Anthropic on 8 Sept 2026 with a
viral warning: "They are racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our
lives"): **"HOW could AI actually kill all humans?"**

The audience: intelligent laypeople who until recently barely followed AI. Many still believe
language models "just predict the next word" and therefore can never be "really" intelligent.
The creator wants arguments that are (a) convincing, (b) easy to grasp, (c) hard to argue against,
(d) accurate — no overclaiming, because a single debunked claim discredits the whole series.

The series has three parts:
1. HOW AI models work and why they can be genuinely competent even though they "only predict the next token".
2. WHY an AI would "want" to exterminate humanity, or do so as a side effect (reward hacking, alignment problems).
3. HOW it could concretely happen — many digestible, plausible examples.

Plus a cross-cutting need: the strongest objections a skeptic will raise, and the best honest rebuttals.

Today's date: 15 September 2026. Use web search aggressively for 2025–2026 material; a lot has
happened recently (new models, new incidents, new papers, new statements). Prefer primary sources
(papers, system cards, original essays, interviews) and always record URLs.

## Your deliverable
Write your findings to the markdown file named in your task (in the scratchpad `research/` folder).
Write in ENGLISH (the final page will be written in Swedish later by a synthesis step).
Be thorough: aim for the 10–25 strongest items in your area, not a shallow list of 50.
Quality bar: every item must be something you could confidently say in a video and defend.

For EACH argument / example / analogy / explanation, use this structure:

### <Short memorable name>
- **Core claim (as you'd say it in a video, 2–5 sentences):** ...
- **Best formulation / quote / analogy:** exact quotes with attribution where possible
- **Who has explained this well (links):** videos (with timestamps if possible), essays, papers, talks, books
- **Evidence (concrete, dated, with URLs):** studies, incidents, numbers, demos
- **Strengths for a lay audience:** why it lands
- **Weaknesses / what a skeptic will say:** the honest counter, and whether it can be rebutted
- **Caveats / accuracy notes:** anything the creator must NOT overclaim
- **Preliminary effectiveness score (1–10) + one-line justification**

At the end of your file add:
- **Top 5 recommendations** for this part (ranked) with one line each on why.
- **Open questions / things you couldn't verify.**
- **Full source list** (URLs).

Return to the orchestrator only a ~15-line summary: what you found, your top 5, and the file path.
Do not paste the full file into your reply.
