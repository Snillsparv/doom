# RED TEAM BRIEFING (read BRIEFING.md first)

You are the RED TEAM for one part of the video series. Several researchers have written notes proposing
arguments, analogies, examples and evidence. Your job is to ATTACK them the way the smartest, most
informed skeptic would — and to make the final product stronger by finding what is wrong, weak,
overclaimed, outdated, duplicated or missing.

Read, in full:
- the research files listed in your task
- research/4_skeptic_objections.md (the skeptic's arsenal)
- research/6_message_effectiveness.md (what evidence says persuades laypeople)

For EVERY proposed item in your part's research files, write:

### <item name> (from <file>)
- **Verdict:** KEEP / STRENGTHEN / DEMOTE / MERGE-WITH <x> / DROP
- **Attack:** the strongest objection a skeptic could make to this specific item (be concrete, cite who makes it and a URL if possible)
- **Accuracy check:** is any claim, number, date, quote or attribution wrong or unverifiable? Verify the 2–3 most important facts with web search and say what you found. Flag anything the creator must not say.
- **Better version?** If a crisper, more defensible, or more vivid formulation exists, give it.
- **Effectiveness estimate (1–10)** for a Swedish lay audience, with one line of reasoning using the rubric in SCHEMA.md.

Then:
- **Missing arguments:** what strong arguments/examples/analogies did the researchers miss? (Do your own searching.)
- **Duplicates / overlaps** across files, and which version to keep.
- **Proposed question structure** for this part: 4–7 viewer questions (in English for now), and for each a ranked list of the items that answer it.
- **Top 3 risks of this part backfiring** (e.g. sounding like sci-fi, overclaiming, jargon) and how to avoid them.

Write to the file named in your task (scratchpad/debate/). Return a ~15-line summary to the orchestrator.
